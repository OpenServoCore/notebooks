# Stage 1. Board

The controller board on its own, with known loads on its motor terminals
and no motor. Every current, voltage and temperature the later stages read
comes through this board's chain, so its conversions are graded here, against
the stage 0 instruments, before anything with a motor is measured. The board
is `dev-v006-2A-D`, the rev 2A board D; its registry entry in
`oscnb/boards.py` carries the parts' nominal values, and the results here
carry the measured conversions, which is where the firmware takes its
constants from.

The sitting needs the board on the DPS, the adapter on its bus at 3 Mbps,
the stage 0 loads (the bare 5 ohm and the series 15 ohm power resistors),
the DMM, the thermocouple and a controlled heat source for the two thermal
experiments: a 3D printer's heated bed, a hot plate with a thermostat or a
filament dryer, anything that holds the whole board at a set temperature.
The `osc` and `osc-lab` tools on the PATH. The board runs the experiment
image (`scripts/build-image.sh --experiment <rev>` in the firmware repo,
then `osc flash` over the bus): open-loop duty, raw streaming and register
access, with the raw current trip and the bus dead-man as its only
protections, so every other rule of a capture is the script's. Datasets land in
`telemetry/<board>/<experiment>/capture-N/`, and each notebook writes
`results/<board>.json` in its folder.

Supply levels are rail volts, VSYS, from a 1S pack's 3.3 V through USB to a
2S pack's 8 V. A swap board has no feed diode and its rail is the battery, so
the levels name the rail, and each capture trims the DPS until the board's
own rail tap reads the level. The DPS output comes up at its previous
setting for a moment when enabled, which the board's rail tolerates, so the
scripts turn it on with the leads connected.

Run in this order. The lap experiments share one wiring and the heated
experiments share another, opamp-temperature reads the ntc-curve result, and
bridge-states comes last on the RL dummy, the bare load in series with two
power inductors.

## TL;DR

The short version, for a reader who wants the numbers before the
notebooks. The table is filled from each experiment's results file, so it
says what the notebooks wrote. Each bound says what it is. A standard error
is statistical, a range is the edges of what was seen, and a class is the
meter's manual. A row whose result carries no bounds shows "-", and its
confidence says why. Every value is board D's. Counts are the ADC's, and
the edges are in timer ticks after the commanded edge, about 0.02 us each.

### Key findings

- The chain at rest is as quiet as the ADC lets it be. Every raw channel
  sits within about a count rms, a milliamp on the current channel and
  3 mV on a terminal, and the one structure is a pattern of about a count
  locked to the 1250 Hz telemetry frame. The converter's own shape is
  larger. It carries a sawtooth of about 2 counts every 256 codes, code 1023
  is about ten codes wide and code 2047 holds about a dozen codes of input,
  so a reading just over a carry reads low.
- The current chain reads 1135.0 counts per amp with an in-drive offset of
  +5.3 counts, from 0.23 to 1.31 A on rails from 3.7 V up. The +/- 1.2 is
  the line's statistical spread, 0.1 %. In absolute terms the scale is
  known to the 20 A jack's 2 % of the reading, and the amplifier times the
  shunt's label reads 1.4 % under the chain. The offset is most likely the
  driver's own current through the shunt. The terminal difference reads its
  nominal ratio to 0.05 %, inside the meter's 0.1 % class. Nothing at a
  3.3 V rail is graded, since the regulator is in dropout there.
- The window floors the board registry carries hold at 3.7 V as well as at
  7.4 V. In slow decay the bridge's ON edge comes about 40 ticks after the
  commanded start, a few ticks earlier on the lower rail, and the shunt is
  inside 1 % about 31 ticks after its half crossing. In fast decay the ON
  edge comes 8 ticks after the commanded start, since no dead time comes
  before it. The OFF edge does not move with the decay. The window's scale
  k is a ratio inside one capture, 1.0063, and a ratio across two captures
  with two wirings is not k.
- On a fixed load the burst method behaves. One 1 mH inductor reads 1.03 to
  1.06 mH by the period fit, two in series add to within 2 %, and the
  fast-decay ramp agrees with the fit within 4 %. The ripple reads 2 to 5 %
  under the fit at 25 % and 5 to 10 % under it at 40 %, a gap of the
  method's own, with high confidence.
- The board NTC's nominal beta of 3950 K is the design conversion. Once the
  rig's two effects are out it reads within 0.2 C of the thermocouple,
  inside the meter's class, and the refitted 4331 K is a correction toward
  a thermocouple that reads low. A fixed-point quadratic through the beta
  equation misses it by up to 5.5 C over 20 to 100 C and 0.7 C over 20 to
  60 C. The current gain falls 0.27 % from 28 to 63 C and comes back, with
  high confidence, and the shunt likely owns about two thirds of it, from
  two sittings a day apart.

### Open questions

- The shunt's value ([current-scale](current-scale/current-scale.ipynb),
  [opamp-temperature](opamp-temperature/opamp-temperature.ipynb)). The
  1.4 % between the amplifier times the label and the chain sits in the
  shunt or in the 20 A jack, and the shunt's share of the gain drift rests
  on two sittings. A 0.1 % reference shunt in series on the millivolt range
  grades the shunt's value and its drift, and both routes in one sitting
  would settle the split.
- The in-drive offset at low current ([current-scale](current-scale/current-scale.ipynb)).
  The two loads' intercepts differ by 4 +/- 2.5 counts. Laps at 0.15 to
  0.32 A, the three stage 0 loads in series, say whether it holds at about
  5 counts or bends toward zero.
- The series step ([current-scale](current-scale/current-scale.ipynb),
  [terminal-chain](terminal-chain/terminal-chain.ipynb)). The series pass
  steps by about 4.5 counts between its 4.5 V and 5.0 V laps, and the
  converter's map takes about a count of it. A load that overlaps both
  ranges, the levels in reverse order, and the current channel walked over
  the same codes would say what the rest is.
- The 3.3 V rail ([current-scale](current-scale/current-scale.ipynb),
  [terminal-chain](terminal-chain/terminal-chain.ipynb)). The lap rows
  carry no reading of the chip's 1.2 V reference. A lap that streams it
  beside the current gives VDD under the lap and what is left after it.
- The rail tap under a lap ([terminal-chain](terminal-chain/terminal-chain.ipynb)).
  The tap reads 31 to 105 mV under the meter during a lap. A meter at the
  tap's own pad against the chip's ground pin says whether that is between
  the probe points or in the chain.
- The bias node's rise ([terminal-chain](terminal-chain/terminal-chain.ipynb)).
  The bare divider predicts 10 mV per volt of leg and this capture reads 6.
  A meter on the bias node during a lap reads the rise directly, and the
  ground term with it.
- The frame pattern ([rest-noise](rest-noise/rest-noise.ipynb)). The taps,
  the rail, the NTC and the chip's own reference share it. A stream with a
  shorter row or a different frame length says whether it follows the
  transmit, and the same streams on a rev 2B board say how much of the
  current channel's share is board D's bodge.
- The earlier ON edge at 3.7 V ([sample-timing](sample-timing/sample-timing.ipynb)).
  Whether it is the bridge's slew crossing half of a smaller swing. A fold
  of the tap's edge in volts at three rails would say.
- The NTC's grade ([ntc-curve](ntc-curve/ntc-curve.ipynb)). The tip taped
  with no paste reads low. The same capture with thermal paste under the
  tip and a firm tape grades the nominal curve to the meter's class, and a
  run with the bed at 100 C grades it above 60 C.
- The burst method's own gap ([bridge-states](bridge-states/bridge-states.ipynb)).
  The converter's carries and the amplifier's step at every slow-decay ON
  edge are the two candidates. Bursts that keep the window's codes inside
  one 256-code block, and a slow burst at a lower rail, separate them.
- The first burst after a change of sign ([bridge-states](bridge-states/bridge-states.ipynb)).
  Whether its jump is the cores' history or the set boundary. Sets of the
  same sign back to back keep the boundary and drop the reversal.

### What the next stage takes from here

Stage 2 reads one motor through this board, so every stage 2 notebook
reads the current and terminal chains from here.

- [rest-resistance](../2-motor/rest-resistance/rest-resistance.ipynb)
  reads the current scale with its bounds, the in-drive offset and the
  current range graded, the terminal scale with its bounds and its offset,
  and the low end of the rail range as the floor of what is graded. It
  quotes the 3.3 V level as ungraded through the current's and the terminal
  difference's excess there and VDD at rest and under the lap. Its board
  temperature is the NTC through the registry's nominal beta, the
  conversion ntc-curve grades.
- [bemf-coast](../2-motor/bemf-coast/bemf-coast.ipynb) reads the current
  scale and the in-drive offset with their bounds, the current range, the
  rest zero for the runs with no rest of their own, the terminal and rail
  tap scales and offsets, both taps' floors at rest, and sample-timing's
  tap ON lag, OFF lag and settle, each at the mean of the two rails.
- [inductance](../2-motor/inductance/inductance.ipynb) reads the current
  scale and offset, the terminal scale and offset, the low end of the rail
  range, and every sample-timing lag and the settle at the mean of the two
  rails. From bridge-states it reads the ripple over the fit for every
  inductive wiring and duty, the fast-decay ramp over the fit, the first
  burst's jump and leftover, and bridge-states' k beside its own.
- [no-load-current](../2-motor/no-load-current/no-load-current.ipynb)
  reads the current scale and the in-drive offset with its bounds, and the
  terminal scale.
- [speed-torque](../2-motor/speed-torque/speed-torque.ipynb) reads the
  current scale, offset and range, the terminal and rail tap scales and
  offsets, and the rail sag per amp.
- [teardown](../2-motor/teardown/teardown.ipynb) reads the current scale
  and offset, the terminal scale and offset, both 3.3 V excesses,
  sample-timing's tap ON lag and OFF lag, and bridge-states' DC resistance
  of the bare resistor and of the resistor with inductor A, with that
  wiring's fast-decay ramp at 25 %, as the two loads its own fit of the
  fast-decay edges runs on.

Fast decay's edges are moving to bridge-states. As of this commit,
bemf-coast and inductance read `fast_edge_lags_ticks` from the teardown's
result, and the teardown fits its own edges and amplifier on bridge-states'
two wirings, until their own corrections land. No stage 2 notebook reads
`counts_per_a_class_pct`, sample-timing's `window_scale`, ntc-curve or
opamp-temperature yet.

## [rest-noise](rest-noise/rest-noise.ipynb)

Targets: the noise floor of each raw channel with the bridge off, and any
structure in it.

Needs: board, DPS, bare load, host.

How: torque-off streams at the control rate, three seconds each, the longest burst the servo arms, three per
level at a 1S, a USB and a 2S level, the board NTC in every row.

Run: `uv run python stages/1-board/rest-noise/capture.py`, then the notebook.

## [current-scale](current-scale/current-scale.ipynb)

Targets: counts per amp and the zero under drive, against the meter.

Needs: board, DPS, bare and series loads, DMM on DC A, host.

How: DC laps at 100 % duty, the DMM in series with the load, at every level.
A line through counts against the meter's amps over both loads. An optional
pass with the DMM across the shunt pads separates the amplifier from the shunt.

Run: `capture.py bare`, `capture.py series`, optionally `capture.py rs1`, then the notebook.

## [terminal-chain](terminal-chain/terminal-chain.ipynb)

Targets: the terminal taps, the rail tap and the bias node against the meter,
and the ADC reference against the chip's own 1.2 V reference, and whether it
holds on a 1S rail.

Needs: board, DPS, bare load, DMM on DC V for three of the four passes, host.

How: the same laps with the meter at one node per pass: across the load,
MOT_A to GND, VSYS to GND. A fine pass with the probes still on VSYS walks
the rail up in 20 mV steps at rest, the DPS set and not trimmed, so the
meter is the x axis: the rail tap's scale on a fine grid, and in its residual
the ADC's own linearity over the codes the tap crosses. The last pass streams
the chip's 1.2 V reference at rest at every level with no meter: VDD = 1.2 V
x 4096 / vref_raw.

Run: `capture.py load`, `capture.py tap_a`, `capture.py rail`, `capture.py rail-fine`, `capture.py vdd`, then the notebook.

## [sample-timing](sample-timing/sample-timing.ipynb)

Targets: the duty from which each crest sample reads the drive honestly,
against the window floors the board registry carries, the shunt's settle inside a window, and the window's scale, the load's V/I inside a window over its V/I at 100 % in the same sitting.

Needs: board, DPS, bare load, host.

How: duty ladders from 3 to 40 % with the 100 % rung as the control, both
directions and both decays at 7.4 V and slow decay again at 3.7 V, with
free-running ADC bursts folded onto the PWM period at each rail.

Run: `uv run python stages/1-board/sample-timing/capture.py`, then the notebook.

## [ntc-curve](ntc-curve/ntc-curve.ipynb)

Targets: the board NTC against the thermocouple, and the count to
temperature conversion the firmware takes from it.

Needs: board on the heater, DPS, thermocouple taped to the board, DMM on temperature, host.

How: the NTC polled while the heater takes the board to about 45 C, then 60 C,
then cools it toward room; the thermocouple logged throughout, and the heater's
own log kept as a side note on the rig, never as a reference.

Run: `uv run python stages/1-board/ntc-curve/capture.py [board]`, once per board of the
revision, then the notebook.

## [opamp-temperature](opamp-temperature/opamp-temperature.ipynb)

Targets: the current chain's gain and zero against board temperature, how
much of that is the shunt's and how much the amplifier's, VDD at each
plateau, and whether the board's own heat under drive moves the NTC.

Needs: board on the heater, DPS, bare load, DMM on DC A off the heater for
the ammeter route or on DC V across the Rs1 pads for the rs1 route, host; the
ntc-curve result.

How: the DC laps at four plateaus of the heater, the NTC in every stream and
a rest stream with the chip's 1.2 V reference before each plateau's laps; a
line per plateau gives the gain and offset, and their slope with temperature
is the drift. The ammeter route reads the chain as a whole, the rs1 route
reads the amplifier alone, and the rest streams read VDD at each plateau.

Run: `capture.py ammeter`, `capture.py rs1`, then the notebook.

## [bridge-states](bridge-states/bridge-states.ipynb)

Targets: the burst inductance method on a load with no moving parts, the
ripple L against the period fit, the fitted R against the DC R, and whether
two inductors add. What the shunt and the taps read in each bridge state
with a real inductive current, in slow and fast decay. The bridge's ON
and OFF edges in fast decay and the shunt amplifier's lag and gain, board
constants fitted through a tick-level model of the bridge on the resistor
and one inductor. The dummy's DC resistance.

Needs: board, DPS, bare load, two 1 mH power inductors (Bourns 2300LL-102-H-RC), A with orange leads and B with brown leads, Dupont pigtails, host.

How: four wirings, each change adding or removing one part. The bare
resistor alone is the control, then come A, A and B in series, and B.
Each wiring gets DC laps at 100 % on a 3.7 and a 4.2 V rail for the DC
resistance, then on a 5 V rail `osc-lab burst` at 25 and 40 %, both signs,
five repeats each, once in slow decay and once in fast, and duty sweeps at
25 and 40 % with the 100 % rung as the control in both decays, the trough
shunt in the row beside the crest samples as the steady-state check. One
inductor's time constant is about 0.19 ms, four PWM periods, and both in
series about 0.37 ms, seven periods. A slow-decay burst runs about 9 periods
past the step, so it ends near 90 % of the final current on one inductor and
near 70 % on both, and fits L from the ramp inside each window and L/R from
the rise across periods. The bare resistor's current settles with the
amplifier, so on it the method should find next to no inductance. Under
slow decay the current circulates through the low-side switches out of the
shunt's path, which the slow bursts see through the whole period. Under
fast decay it returns to the rail through the body diodes and reaches zero
before the next drive below 50 %, so the fast bursts are periodic from the
first full period and show the turn-off, the return on the taps, the amplifier's
tail and the discontinuous current inside one period.

Run: `uv run python stages/1-board/bridge-states/capture.py`, then the notebook.
