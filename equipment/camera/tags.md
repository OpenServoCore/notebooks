# Tags and calibration board

The camera measures angle from two printed ArUco tags, and its lens is
calibrated from a printed ChArUco board. Both sheets are generated by
[tags.py](tags.py), from the tag and board definitions in
[oscnb/fiducial.py](../../oscnb/fiducial.py). The PDFs are not committed. From
the repo root, this writes them into this folder:

```
uv run python equipment/camera/tags.py
```

- `tags-angle.pdf`: the HORN tag (id 1) and the REFERENCE tag (id 2),
  each a 25 mm black square with a 50 mm check square and rulers beside it,
  plus four spares.
- `tags-calibration.pdf`: a 7 by 10 ChArUco board with 25 mm squares, used
  only for the lens calibration.

## Printing

1. Print both sheets on US Letter at **100 %, "Actual size"**. "Fit to page"
   shrinks them by a few percent.
2. A laser printer is best. Inkjet ink on plain paper can smear and makes
   the paper wrinkle when it is glued.
3. Measure each tag's 50 mm check square along X and along Y with calipers
   or a ruler, outer edge to outer edge, and five squares of the board along
   X and along Y. The experiment that films the tags asks for these six
   numbers, which give the real scale of each print. A
   correct print at 100 % measures 50 mm within 1 mm, and on the board five
   squares measure 125 mm within 2.5 mm. Anything further off means the page
   was scaled, usually by "Fit to page", so reprint at "Actual size". The
   angle itself hardly depends on the print's scale: even a 2 mm stretch in
   one direction moves it by about 0.02 degrees.

## The tags

1. Cut each tag out along its dashed line. The white border inside the line
   stays on: the detector needs that white margin around the black square.
2. Glue the tags flat with a glue stick, spray adhesive or double-sided
   tape. Wet glue such as white glue makes the paper ripple, and a rippled
   tag reads the wrong angle.
3. Where they go depends on the experiment. Stage 0 camera-tag glues both
   on one rigid plate, about 60 mm apart, with their edges square to each
   other. Stage 3 puts the HORN tag on the servo horn and the REFERENCE tag
   on the servo body.

## The calibration board

Mount the whole board sheet flat on something rigid, such as foam board, a
clipboard or the backing board of a picture frame, or tape its corners flat
to the desk. The lens calibration assumes a perfectly flat board, so
flatness matters more here than anywhere else. Keep it out from behind
glass, whose reflections hide the codes.

### Filming it

The calibration needs the board seen from many angles and in every part of
the frame, at the same focus the tag clips use, and from the same lens: a
phone's stabilisation moves the lens while the phone turns in the hand, and
a calibration fitted to a hand-held clip does not describe the lens the tag
clips see from the mount. So the phone stays on its mount and the board
moves:

1. Set up the phone over the tags at the experiment's working distance and
   lock focus and exposure there. The phone does not move again.
2. Tape the board sheet to a piece of cardboard, so it can be propped
   without bending. Paper on its own cannot.
3. Record 30 to 60 s. Move the board under the camera and hold it still for
   about two seconds at each pose: flat on the desk in the middle, then slid
   to each corner and each edge of the frame; then propped on a book at 20
   to 30 degrees with the raised side facing up, down, left and right in
   turn, at two or three places each. Fifteen to twenty poses. The board
   need not fit in the frame; a dozen of its corners in view is enough.
   Holding the board in the hands at the working distance, on its
   cardboard backing, works as well, as long as each pose is held still for
   a couple of seconds and the phone stays on its mount.
4. Film the tag clips with the same lock and at the same distance. The
   extraction takes one frame per pause of the board clip.

The calibration belongs to the focus it was filmed at. If a later setup
changes the lens to tag distance by more than about a third, film a new
board clip there.
